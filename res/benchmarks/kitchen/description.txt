The scenario unfolds in a kitchen where a chef is tasked with creating exquisite dishes for their customers. The chef's goal is to craft meals that align with the preferences of their clientele. To facilitate this, the instance file must present at least one recipe for the chef to consider. If multiple recipes are available, the chef has the discretion to select which one to prepare. Each customer has a curated list of preferred recipes, and they are required to accept at least one dish from this selection, although the chef is initially unaware of the specific dish each customer will favor.

To assemble dishes according to the recipes, the chef relies on the ingredients available in the kitchen's inventory. The instance file details the ingredients consumed by each recipe, alongside those present in the kitchen's stock and their respective quantities.

Upon completing a dish, the chef offers it to the customers who have expressed a liking for it. A customer can decline the dish, provided they have other preferred dishes listed. If a dish is rejected, the chef may present it to another customer with a preference for that dish, proceeding in this manner until the dish is either accepted by a customer or rejected by all customers with a preference for it. Acceptance of a dish results in a satisfied customer.

For instance, consider two customers, John and Marie, with John enjoying both pasta and cake, while Marie has a preference solely for pasta. The optimal strategy for the chef would be to initially prepare pasta and offer it to John, who has the option to decline. If John rejects the pasta, it can then be offered to Marie, who must accept it due to her singular preference. Subsequently, the chef can prepare a cake for John, which he must accept. This approach ensures all customers are satisfied. Conversely, if the chef first prepares a cake for John and he rejects it, and there is insufficient flour to prepare two servings of pasta, the chef fails to satisfy all customers.
